# Tester invitations (drafts)

Edit freely. Keep the ask small and give an easy way out.

## Short message to a work acquaintance

> Hi [name], I built a side project outside of work, a privacy-focused AI workspace that checks for sensitive info like card numbers before a chat message is sent. Before I share it more widely I would like two or three people to try it and tell me what is confusing or broken. It takes about 20 minutes, there is nothing to install, and it uses made-up data only. Would you be up for that? No worries at all if not. If yes, I will send a link and a one-page guide.

## Follow-up to someone you already contacted (after a week or two)

> Hi [name], following up on my note from earlier. No pressure, and I understand if you are busy. If you do have 20 minutes, there is a sample site that needs no sign-up, and I only need your honest first reaction. If now is not a good time, just say so and I will not ask again.

## Message to send with the guide

> Thank you! The guide is here: [link to docs/tester-guide.md on GitHub]. Part 1 is all you need, and it is the sample site with made-up data. Please do not enter anything real. Short, rough notes are great, and "I got confused here" is exactly what I am looking for. No deadline.

## GitHub issue: "Looking for a few early testers"

> I am looking for two or three early testers before a wider release. The [tester guide](docs/tester-guide.md) has a 20-minute walk-through of the sample site (no install) and an optional Docker install for those who like that. Please use made-up data only. Comment here or email hello@securemind.chat if you would like to help. Security problems go through [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not this issue.

## A note for you

- Ask for a specific small thing, a time estimate and an easy "no". That makes saying yes simpler.
- Two replies is a successful test round. Fix what they find before you ask anyone else.
- Thank each tester after they reply, and tell them what you changed because of what they said.
